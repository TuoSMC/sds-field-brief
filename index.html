Cloudian: object access and retention

OSNEXUS: choose a pool architecture

SteelDome: compute and storage together
HyperSERV combines StratiSERV virtualization and StratiSTOR storage on common nodes. Test CPU, memory and drive contention, failure behavior and expansion. StratiSTOR can also be deployed separately; the radar includes the selected HCI model.

Use $115k as the working assumption. The chart checks stability under different methods; it does not establish a vendor quote or fair-market price. The green shaded interval shows uncertainty in the typical value, given this archive and model. It is not the range of the next customer’s bill.

Open the formulas · normalization, Bayesian bootstrap & robust fit

1 / Put every subscription on a comparable term

ai = Si × 12 / mi,   zi = ln(ai),   i = 1,…,11

S = original subscription selling price; m = contract months; a = annual-equivalent amount. This comparison removes term length. It does not remove capacity, product, discount or support differences. A one-month price × 12 does not establish 12 renewals.

2 / Bayesian bootstrap: let the weight of each order vary

w(b) ∼ Dirichlet(1,…,1)
f(w) = Γ(Σ αi) / ∏ Γ(αi) · ∏ wiαᵢ − 1,   Σ wi = 1
Fb(x) = Σ wi(b) 𝟙(ai ≤ x)
θb = inf {x : Fb(x) ≥ 0.5}

Draw 100,000 sets of order weights; calculate the weighted median each time. Seed 20260925; NumPy default_rng / PCG64. P5 / P50 / P95 of θ are $56,962.50 / $114,796 / $126,456. Round P50 to the nearest $5,000: $115,000. Rounding is a presentation choice. More draws stabilize computation; they do not add customer evidence. Method: Rubin, The Bayesian Bootstrap (1981).

3 / Robust crosscheck: reduce the influence of extreme orders

zi ∼ tν=4(μ, σ)
(μ̂, σ̂) = arg minμ, σ>0 [11 ln σ + 5/2 Σ ln(1 + (zi − μ)² / (4σ²))]
probust = exp(μ̂) = $101,639.63

Maximum likelihood on log annual amounts; ν = 4 is fixed as a heavy-tail assumption. μ̂ = 11.52919; σ̂ = 0.85861. Sensitivity ν = 3 or 7 gives $101,564 or $102,458. These are typical centers, not dollar means. An untruncated log-Student-t has no finite dollar mean; it is not used to calculate expected revenue. Method reference: Student-t robust noise models.

4 / Translate the fee into a commercial scenario

N(q) = 5 partners × 2 RFQs/week × 48 weeks × q
B(q, p) = N(q) × p
B(30%, $115,000) = 144 × $115,000 = $16,560,000
Market-size equivalent = B / $24,270,000,000 × 100 = 0.0682%

All future wins are assumed to carry one new 12-month subscription at p. The median-informed fee is an assigned scenario value; it is not an estimate of the arithmetic mean across all future orders. Volume and win rate are assumptions. Bookings are neither recognized revenue nor profit.

Scenario assumptions

  • The 6,000+ listed-company pool and state/industry SMB resources are presenter-reported resources, before deduplication and qualification. They are not interested buyers or open orders.
  • Five partners each contribute two unique qualified RFQs per week: 10 per week, 40 per four-week planning month. Alternative BOMs and RFQ revisions belong to the same opportunity.
  • At 30%, each 40-RFQ cohort yields 12 eventual PO wins. Twelve wins per planning month assumes sustained inflow and a mature pipeline after the actual closing lag. Split POs do not create additional wins.
  • The annual plan uses 12 four-week periods: 48 selling weeks, allowing four weeks outside the selling cadence. A full 52-week sensitivity at the same cadence yields 156 wins and $17.94M in modeled SDS subscription bookings; neither case represents actual bookings or recognized revenue. Annual quota coverage requires the actual target.
  • Each modeled win assumes one new 12-month SDS subscription at the selected fee. The default $115,000 is a rounded typical-value hypothesis; alternatives use annual-equivalent mean and mean + SD. Hardware, separately priced professional services and unallocated bundles are excluded. Subscription-SKU pricing can include inseparable support. These modeled bookings are not ARR, margin or recognized revenue.
