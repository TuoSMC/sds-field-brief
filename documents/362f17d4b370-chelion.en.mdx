## Problem

In 2022 Chelion, a Chinese battery-storage maker, opened Chelion America in Santa Clara to bring two product lines into the United States: the iHome residential system (a single-phase hybrid inverter from 7.6 to 12 kW with stackable 5 kWh battery modules) and a commercial and industrial line of air- and liquid-cooled cabinets. I joined as Inside Sales Manager and co-developed the residential battery with project and product management responsibilities, while building the customer metrics and reporting the sales team ran on.

A product that works in China is not a product in California. It needs US electrical and EMC certification, a configuration that installers will quote, a landed cost that survives tariff and port charges, and packaging that fills a container without damage.

## Constraints

Certification came first in the plan: the hybrid inverter line carried an FCC Part 15 Subpart B declaration, signed in February 2023, and UL listing was scheduled into the delivery plan. Pricing had to be built from the port backwards, not from the factory forwards: freight, port fees, tariff at two different rates for inverter and battery, drayage, warehouse. Two West Coast gateways were modelled against each other. Every unconfirmed input was marked in red so that nobody quoted a guess. Chelion also evaluated AlphaESS's SMILE line as a distributor in 2022, which is how I first worked with the product family I would later launch at AlphaESS.

## My judgment

I treated the residential product as three linked designs, not one: the system configuration (10 kW inverter with two, three or four 5 kWh modules), the container plan (how many inverters and batteries fit on how many pallets), and the price structure (three margin tiers by committed volume). Changing one changed the others, so they were kept in one workbook and one delivery plan.

On the C&I side I took part in headquarters' reviews of the 2023 C&I battery programme. The programme documents specify 480 V AC output for the US grid, certification as a scheduled phase, and an air-cooling versus liquid-cooling trade study, the same questions that later became the core of my AlphaESS work.

## Action

Between November 2022 and May 2023: brought the first iHome demo kit (one 12 kW inverter, three 5 kWh modules, display shells) from Shanghai to Santa Clara for showroom and trade-show use; prepared the FCC Supplier's Declaration of Conformity covering all five inverter SKUs, signed in February 2023; defined pallet and 40-foot container plans for the 10 kW/10 kWh, 10 kW/15 kWh and 10 kW/20 kWh systems; built the FOB-to-DDP landed-cost model with the headquarters pricing team, then the three-tier retail pricing and a delivery plan by set for the North American launch. In parallel I reviewed headquarters' C&I roadmap, feasibility study and configuration matrix, and issued the requirements for a 2 MW / 3.5 MWh solar-plus-storage proposal in Contra Costa County.

## Verifiable output

The FCC declaration, the demo-kit customs pack, the container plans and the pricing workbooks exist as dated documents and can be shown with figures redacted. The C&I brochure for North America (January 2023) is a finished piece of collateral. What this case claims is the design and project work up to launch readiness in mid-2023; unit sales after that point are outside it.
