## Problem

In 2023 AlphaESS was taking two product lines into new markets: the STORION-LC-200 liquid-cooled C&I cabinet (372.7 kWh, 186 kW at 0.5C, eight 46.6 kWh liquid-cooled modules) worldwide, and the SMILE-SP/SPB residential series (7.6 / 9.6 kW hybrid or AC-coupled, 8.2 kWh modules expandable to 49.2 kWh, 240 V / 60 Hz, EPS backup) in North America. I took part in project and product design and ran project management for both lines, alongside North American business development.

North America was not a translation job. Installers wanted a US-certified unit that could run backup. C&I customers wanted a system that, with the EMS, could do peak shaving, demand-charge management and backup. The company needed pricing and a channel that still worked after California's NEM 3.0.

## Constraints

Certification was a gate, not a feature: more than half of first-contact calls ended on the lack of a US certificate. I reported that back and set up a bi-weekly certification and policy watch, which fixed the order of work. Liquid cooling had to be argued with numbers: a system-wide temperature difference within 3 °C, fan-less operation, an IP54 outdoor cabinet, 1500 V DC with up to ten cabinets per combiner. Residential converged on the North American configuration: 7.6 kW per unit, 60 kW maximum parallel, four MPPTs, AFCI and rapid shutdown, backup defined as a 60-second peak. Internal prices, margins and customer names stay out of this case.

## My judgment

I treated the regional product-manager role as the intake for requirements rather than a pass-through for orders. AlphaESS's C&I intake asks the regional PM for system capacity, PCS power, load profile, grid voltage and frequency, point of interconnection, genset control by the EMS, fire suppression, supply model, delivery and warranty. That frame set the level of detail at which I took part in design and project management for North America.

On channel, I judged that the first C&I scenario to close would be EV-fleet charging paired with storage, and proposed turning second-tier storage makers into OEM/ODM partners rather than competing head-on. On residential, I judged that after NEM 3.0 the pitch had to move from bill savings to backup and self-consumption, so the NEM 3.0 analysis came before the installer scripts.

## Action

Between August and November 2023 I produced the North America C&I / utility BESS market research and an NREL-based cost benchmark for ODM negotiation; re-cut the HQ C&I decks into US training material covering the LC-200 architecture, module, BMS and EMS scenarios; wrote the US presentation script including the LC-200 product section; presented the C&I US market insight report; built the SMILE-SPB landed-cost pricing model and a US residential storage competitor database; completed the NEM 3.0 impact analysis, installer pitch scripts and a 1,681-contact installer list; and stood up a self-hosted CRM with more than 2,500 contacts. I also took part in a C&I solution discussion for a Chilean fuel-retail operator.
